Understanding Ice Tilt

What the NHL stat is

Ice Tilt is an NHL EDGE IQ stat built with AWS. The league's tracking system records every skater, goalie and the puck about 100 times a second. Ice Tilt averages where everyone is on the ice over a rolling two-minute window, so it answers one question: which team is playing in the other team's end right now, and by how much.

During stoppages the official number slowly drifts back toward neutral. It appears in broadcast graphics, but the league does not publish it as a stat you can look up, which is why you only find articles about it.

What this tracker does

You act as the tracking system. Each second the clock runs, the tracker records where you last placed the play. It then applies the same two-minute rolling average, resets it each period, and lets it drift to neutral while the whistle is blown.

One position from you is coarser than twelve tracked bodies, so the size of these numbers won't match a broadcast graphic. The direction and the swings will. The NHL hasn't published its exact scale.

How to read the number

0–9Even. Play is trading ends.
10–24Leaning. One team is winning more battles.
25–44Tilted. Sustained pressure, often after a few won draws or an icing.
45+Hemmed in. The defending team can't get it out. Watch for a goal or a tired line.

A positive number in the chart favors the team on top of the chart (Team A). The territorial share is the same number shown as a split of the ice: +30 means a 65 / 35 split.

Things to watch for

  • Tilt often flips right after a goal. A team that is ahead tends to sit back, so the trailing team's tilt climbs. Analysts call this score effects.
  • Tilt is not shots. A team can carry a big tilt and still lose the shot count if the defense keeps everything to the outside.
  • Power plays tilt the ice heavily by design. Note them so you don't read a man-advantage as momentum.
  • Long stretches above 30 near the end of a shift usually mean the defending line is stuck on the ice.
