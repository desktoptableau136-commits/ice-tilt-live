How this number is made

The official stat

The NHL and AWS compute Ice Tilt from player and puck tracking at about 100 positions per second. It averages where everyone is over a rolling two-minute window. That tracking feed is private and only reaches TV broadcast graphics.

This version

The public NHL game feed logs every shot, missed shot, blocked shot, hit, faceoff, takeaway, giveaway, goal and penalty with its spot on the ice, roughly one every 10–15 seconds. The page treats each event's spot as where play was until the next event, then averages that over the last two minutes of game clock. The average restarts each period. Stoppages don't count because the game clock is stopped.

The direction and the swings track the official idea closely. The size of the number won't match a broadcast graphic, and quiet stretches with no events hold the last known spot.

Reading it

0–9Even. Play is trading ends.
10–24Leaning. One team is winning more battles.
25–44Tilted. Sustained pressure.
45+Hemmed in. The defending team can't clear it.
  • Power plays (shaded on the chart) tilt the ice by design.
  • The trailing team usually gains tilt late in games as the leader sits back.
  • Tilt is not shots. Compare the two in the period table.
