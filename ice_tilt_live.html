How this number is made

The official stat

The NHL and AWS compute Ice Tilt from player and puck tracking at about 100 positions per second. It averages where everyone is over a rolling two-minute window. That tracking feed is private and only reaches TV broadcast graphics.

This version

The public NHL game feed logs every shot, missed shot, blocked shot, hit, faceoff, takeaway, giveaway, goal and penalty with its spot on the ice, roughly one every 10–15 seconds. The page treats each event's spot as where play was until the next event, then averages that over the last two minutes of game clock. The average restarts each period. Stoppages don't count because the game clock is stopped.

The direction and the swings track the official idea closely. The size of the number won't match a broadcast graphic, and quiet stretches with no events hold the last known spot.

Reading it

The number is how far the average spot of play sits from center ice toward a goal line: 0 at center, about 28 at the blue line, 100 at the goal line.

0–9Even. Play is centered around the red line, and both teams are getting time in the other end.
10–24Leaning. Play is drifting toward one end, but on average it's still in the neutral zone.
25–44Tilted. On average play is at or just past one blue line. One team is spending most of its time in the other end.
45+Dominant. On average play is deep in one zone. For two minutes the defending team has barely gotten out.
  • The 5 and 10 minute boxes use the same method over longer windows. When a short window disagrees with a long one, a team is pushing against the run of play.
  • Power plays (PP) and a pulled goalie (EN) are shaded on the chart. Both are a man advantage, so they tilt the ice by design.
  • The trailing team usually gains tilt late in games as the leader sits back. When that's happening, the verdict says so and a Score effect tag appears next to the number.
  • Score-adjusted (Tilt menu, top bar) takes that push away. Every game of the 2025–26 regular season was run through this same method to find the average tilt for a team down 1, or down 2 or more, at each minute of regulation. That expected tilt is subtracted second by second, so a team that's merely chasing reads near even, and a team out-playing the situation still stands out. Home ice is averaged out. The rink always shows raw position.
  • Tilt is not shots. Compare the two in the period table.
